# Example: Selective Scope Language

**Dimension**: Selective scope language

**Definition**: How narrowly or broadly rules, standards, or harms are defined relative to the target. High scores reflect broad, elastic terms applied with explicit or implicit carve-outs, or heavy emphasis on context/intent/speaker identity as decisive.

**Few-shot examples** (optional to include):

Example A (score 4):
Text: "While the standard applies generally, heightened scrutiny is warranted when the communication originates from sources with a documented history of undermining public trust. Context and speaker intent must be weighed carefully in such cases."
Evidence: ["heightened scrutiny is warranted when the communication originates from sources...", "Context and speaker intent must be weighed carefully"]
Score: 4
Rationale: Elastic application conditioned on speaker history and intent creates a clear selective scope.

Example B (score 1):
Text: "The rule applies equally to all parties based on the content of the communication alone."
Evidence: ["The rule applies equally to all parties based on the content of the communication alone."]
Score: 1
Rationale: Explicitly rejects identity-based differentiation; only residual possibility of unstated exceptions.
